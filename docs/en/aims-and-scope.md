# Aims and scope

**Journal of AI-Assisted Research for Life Sciences**

**Status:** draft for adoption (version 0.1, 14 November 2026). This text takes effect when the publisher adopts it.

**Language:** English · [Wersja polska](../pl/aims-and-scope.md)

## 1. Purpose

The *Journal of AI-Assisted Research for Life Sciences* is a gold open-access journal. It publishes empirical research in the life sciences, with a focus on health and sport science.

The journal exists for work in which the study was done by people. Generative tools may help write the manuscript from the real data. They do not replace the study, the authors, or the editor's decision.

Readers should be able to see what was done, who is accountable, which tools were used, and where the raw data are. That is the journal's answer to unverified mass-generated text ("AI slop") and to predatory publishing.

## 2. Scope

The journal considers work in:

- sport science, exercise science, biomechanics, training, and physical performance;
- sports medicine, rehabilitation, and injury research;
- physical activity and public health;
- human biology and physiology relevant to health or performance;
- clinical medicine and allied health, where the question fits the journal;
- pharmacy and pharmacology, where the study is empirical and ethically approved;
- biotechnology, molecular and cellular biology, and related laboratory science;
- environmental science and environmental health, where the work bears on living systems or human health.

Interdisciplinary manuscripts are welcome when the question, the methods, and the data are in scope and a specialist reviewer can be found.

The publication language of articles is English. A Polish title and abstract may be added. Policies are published in English and Polish.

## 3. What "AI-assisted" means here

Authors may use artificial intelligence to help prepare a manuscript **from data, code, and findings that humans produced and checked**. Typical uses include drafting text, editing language, translating, writing analysis code that a human reviews, and building plots from the real dataset.

The study itself is human work: design, collection, measurement, imaging, fieldwork, interviews, and the analysis the authors stand behind. A tool that writes a plausible paper without that study is outside the journal's scope.

AI is never an author. Named humans take responsibility for the content, the numbers, the citations, and the interpretation, in line with the [Committee on Publication Ethics (COPE)](https://publicationethics.org/) and the [International Committee of Medical Journal Editors (ICMJE)](https://www.icmje.org/).

Full rules: [AI Use and Disclosure Policy](ai-use-and-disclosure-policy.md).

## 4. Conditions for empirical work

Every empirical submission must meet all of the following:

1. The experiment, collection, or analysis was actually carried out by humans. A reanalysis of an existing dataset is eligible when the authors had the right to use it and the source is identified.
2. AI did not generate or modify primary data.
3. Raw data are deposited in a public repository, with a documented exception only where the law or participant privacy makes full public release impossible. Editors and reviewers still receive access. See [Author guidelines](author-guidelines.md).
4. Ethics approval, informed consent, animal-welfare approval, permits, and trial or protocol registration are reported where they apply.
5. A completed AI disclosure statement is submitted even when no AI tool was used.
6. Primary-data images (microscopy, blots, gels, photographs of samples, instrument scans, clinical images) are original captures. Plots drawn from the real data may be AI-assisted if that use is disclosed and a human has checked them.

## 5. Article types

| Type | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Original research | A full report of a human-conducted empirical study. |
| Brief report | A shorter report of a complete, focused empirical study. |
| Systematic review or meta-analysis | A review whose search, screening, extraction, and synthesis were done by humans. |
| Methods article | A method the authors developed and tested on real data or on a simulation they actually ran and labelled as a simulation. |
| Data note | A description of a deposited dataset from human-conducted collection, written so that others can reuse it. |
| Letter | A short comment on an article published in this journal. |

Study protocols and registered reports may be added after the editorial board adopts a procedure for them. They are not accepted under this draft.

## 6. What the journal does not publish

- Manuscripts whose results, measurements, images of primary data, or participant quotations were generated by a model.
- Simulations presented as real-world observations.
- Papers that list a model, a company, or a software product as an author.
- Submissions without the AI disclosure statement, without a data availability statement, or without the ethics statements the study design requires.
- Work that is outside the scope above, including purely speculative essays with no documented inquiry.
- Duplicate publication, paper-mill output, and manuscripts offered with a guarantee of acceptance.

A human editor may decline a submission at triage when it is outside the scope or does not meet the minimum requirements. That decision is not delegated to a model.

## 7. Access, fees, and decisions

The working model is gold open access. There is no submission fee. A fixed article processing charge (APC) of 1000 PLN is charged only after the article is accepted. The charge never influences the editorial decision, and editors and reviewers deciding on a paper do not see payment status. There are no fee waivers for inability to pay.

Reviewers who complete a review receive a discount voucher toward the APC of their own future article. The discount is set by the editorial office and published on the site.

The working licence for articles is [Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0)](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Authors retain copyright. These are draft defaults; the publisher confirms them before the policies are adopted. See the [launch roadmap](launch-roadmap.md).

Peer review has two rounds. Round 1 is an AI preliminary review, labelled as such. Round 2 is review by human experts. A human editor makes the final decision. See the [Peer review policy](peer-review-policy.md).

The journal does not sell authorship, citations, or decisions. It does not display metrics it has not earned.
