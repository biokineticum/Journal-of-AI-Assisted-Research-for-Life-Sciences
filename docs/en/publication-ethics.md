# Publication ethics

**Journal of AI-Assisted Research for Life Sciences**

**Status:** draft for adoption (version 0.1, 14 November 2026).

**Language:** English · [Wersja polska](../pl/publication-ethics.md)

This policy is the journal's commitment to the Committee on Publication Ethics (COPE), including the COPE Core Practices, and to the International Committee of Medical Journal Editors (ICMJE) Recommendations. Where a case needs a procedure, the editor uses the relevant COPE flowchart. Those documents remain the procedural reference if this page is quieter than the flowchart.

Allegations and complaints go to the editor-in-chief at [EMAIL].

## 1. What the journal expects

Published work rests on real inquiry, readable data, and named humans who answer for it. Generative tools may assist writing only inside the [AI Use and Disclosure Policy](ai-use-and-disclosure-policy.md).

The journal does not sell decisions. It does not guarantee acceptance. It does not display an impact factor, a ranking, or an index membership it does not hold. It does not invite authors with false claims about speed, indexing, or editorial board membership.

## 2. Duties of authors

Authors:

- present an accurate account of the work and an objective discussion of its importance;
- can show the raw data behind the results and deposit the human-collected dataset and the analysis code as the [Data availability and FAIR policy](data-availability-and-fair-policy.md) requires, with a Data Availability Statement, and with access for the AI preliminary review and the human reviewers at submission;
- submit original work, not a manuscript that is under review elsewhere or already published as an article (preprints are allowed);
- disclose AI use in the mandatory statement, or state that no AI tool was used;
- disclose competing interests and funding;
- report ethics approval, consent, animal welfare, permits, and registration where they apply;
- tell the editor promptly if they find an error after submission or publication;
- respond to editorial queries about integrity.

The corresponding author is responsible for communication and confirms that all co-authors have approved the manuscript and the disclosure. Approval does not move accountability off any co-author. ICMJE criterion 4 applies to each author.

## 3. Authorship and contributorship

Authors are humans who meet all four ICMJE criteria, set out in the [AI Use and Disclosure Policy](ai-use-and-disclosure-policy.md). AI tools are not authors.

Gift authorship, guest authorship, ghost authorship, and omission of a person who meets the criteria are misconduct. Disputes about who qualifies are the authors' responsibility to resolve before acceptance. The editor may pause the process and, if the dispute is not resolved, ask an institution to decide who can be an author. The editor does not adjudicate employment disputes beyond what is needed to protect the record.

Changes to the author list after submission require written agreement from every author, including those added or removed, and an explanation. Changes after publication require a published correction and the same agreement, unless a finding of misconduct makes that agreement impossible. The editor then records the reason.

CRediT roles are published. A person who contributed but does not meet authorship criteria is named in the Acknowledgements, with their agreement where the journal can reasonably seek it.

## 4. Data, images, and research misconduct

The following are misconduct:

- **Fabrication** — inventing data, images, quotations, or results, including generation of those materials by an AI tool, and a data figure or statistical plot produced as a generated image and presented as the output of the analysis.
- **Falsification** — altering primary data, images, or records so that the report misleads, including undisclosed splicing, generative enhancement, selective deletion, or a number, statistic, or conclusion that does not match the deposited dataset.
- **Plagiarism** — presenting another person's text, ideas, or data as one's own, including unattributed text taken from a model that reproduced a source.
- **Undisclosed AI use** — any use covered by the AI policy that is absent from, or misrepresented in, the AI Disclosure Statement.
- **Citation manipulation** — fabricated references, irrelevant citations inserted to inflate counts, and coerced citation.
- **Unethical research** — work that required approval, consent, or registration and does not have it, and work misdescribed as approved.
- **Duplicate submission or publication**, and salami publication that hides the overlap.
- **Paper-mill submission**, fabricated peer review, and false reviewer identities.

Zero tolerance for undisclosed AI use means the journal always treats the breach as misconduct and always records the outcome. It does not mean that every case ends in the same sanction. The remedy follows the effect on the scholarly record (section 11). A minor language edit that was left off the form is still a breach. It is not ignored because the science survived.

Authors remain responsible for model output they submit. "The tool wrote it" is not a defence to a fabricated citation, a fabricated number, or a plagiarised paragraph.

Image files may be examined for manipulation. Authors supply original captures when asked, and the code that builds each data figure. Failure to supply them when the authors should have them is treated as an integrity concern.

A statement that the data are available, when the identifier does not resolve or the reviewers cannot open the files at submission, is a breach. Withholding the dataset from the AI preliminary review or from the human reviewers is an incomplete submission and, where the statement says otherwise, an integrity concern. The rule is the [Data availability and FAIR policy](data-availability-and-fair-policy.md). Sensitive health data remain as open as possible and as closed as necessary: a controlled-access route is lawful; a missing deposit is not.

## 5. Human participants, animals, and safety

Research involving human participants, identifiable data, or human material complies with the Declaration of Helsinki and with the approval and consent requirements in the [Author guidelines](author-guidelines.md). Animal research follows the 3Rs and ARRIVE 2.0, and it has the required committee approval.

Participant privacy survives publication. Identifiable images and quotations need specific consent. De-identification is real: removing a name from a spreadsheet while leaving a combination of variables that identifies a person in a small sample is not sufficient. The editor may require a smaller public deposit and a controlled-access route.

The journal does not publish work whose purpose is to evade anti-doping rules. Research on health, analytical methods, or prevention may be in scope when it has a legitimate scientific aim and the necessary approvals.

Laboratory and field work meets the biosafety, biosecurity, and permit rules that apply to it. Missing legally required approval is a reason to reject and, where people may be at risk, a reason to contact the institution.

## 6. Competing interests

Everyone in the process discloses interests that a reasonable reader could think relevant.

- **Authors** declare financial interests (employment, consulting, equity, patents, paid testimony, funding) and non-financial interests (a close relationship, a strong public stance, a role in a body whose product is assessed).
- **Reviewers** decline the invitation when they cannot be fair, and they declare borderline interests so the editor can decide.
- **Editors** do not handle manuscripts in which they are authors, or in which a funder, a relative, a close collaborator, or a financial interest makes a fair process unreasonable.

A competing interest is not, by itself, a reason to reject a sound paper. Concealment is the problem. Undisclosed interests that emerge after publication are corrected publicly and may lead to a new review of the decision.

Staff and board members do not use confidential submissions to train models, to trade, or to advantage their own unpublished work.

## 7. Overlap, preprints, and citations

**Preprints** on a recognised server are allowed. The cover letter links them. The version of record is the article the journal publishes.

**Prior publication** of the same results as a journal article, or submission to two journals at once, is not allowed. Conference abstracts and theses are not prior publication; the manuscript says when the work was previously presented in those forms.

**Overlap** with the authors' other papers is disclosed in the cover letter, with copies for the editor. Reanalysis of a public dataset cites the source and states what is new.

**Citations** exist and support the claims. Authors do not pad reference lists. Reviewers do not require citations to their own work as a condition of a favourable report. Editors do not require citations to the journal as a condition of acceptance.

## 8. Duties of reviewers

Reviewers follow the [Peer review policy](peer-review-policy.md). In particular they:

- provide an objective, reasoned report, or decline promptly;
- keep the manuscript confidential, and do not upload the manuscript, the data, or the code to any external or third-party AI service;
- disclose competing interests;
- report suspected fabrication, falsification, plagiarism, undisclosed AI use, or unethical research to the editor, and do not investigate on their own beyond the files they were given;
- do not use information from the review for their own work until it is public.

A reviewer who used a model at all ran it locally, and tells the editor the model name and version. Uploading the manuscript or the data to an external AI service is a breach of confidentiality. The reviewer signs the intellectual judgement with their own name inside the editorial system. A model is not the reviewer.

## 9. Duties of editors

Editors:

- decide on the basis of the work's quality, ethics, and fit to the scope, without regard to the authors' nationality, institution, gender, or seniority, except where a competing interest requires another editor;
- keep a human in charge of triage outcomes, of any query raised by the AI preliminary review, and of the final decision;
- ensure that the AI preliminary review ran only on open-weight models hosted on infrastructure the journal controls, that no manuscript, dataset, or code was sent to an external AI API, and that a human checked the report before it was sent;
- invite reviewers who can assess the work and who are not blocked by a conflict;
- maintain confidentiality;
- act on integrity concerns even when a paper has already been published, and even when the authors, a reviewer, or a board member would prefer silence;
- publish corrections, expressions of concern, and retractions in a form that is linked to the article and is free to read;
- keep a record of every tool, model, and version used in the AI preliminary review, of the infrastructure they ran on, and of the human-checked report. The report is shared with the human reviewers and the editor. It never makes the decision;
- do not overturn a sound negative decision because a funder, a sponsor, or the publisher prefers acceptance.

**Editorial independence.** The publisher and any funder of the journal's operations do not overrule the editor on acceptance or rejection of a manuscript. The legal publisher remains responsible for the title under Polish press law once the title is registered. That responsibility is not a right to insert or remove scientific conclusions.

**Editors as authors.** An editor's own submission is handled by another editor. The author-editor does not see the reviewers' names if the review is anonymised, and does not take part in the decision.

## 10. Handling allegations

The editor-in-chief, [EDITOR-IN-CHIEF NAME], or a deputy named in the masthead if the editor-in-chief is conflicted, handles allegations. The procedure follows COPE's flowcharts. The usual steps are:

1. **Record** the allegation, the date, and the evidence offered. Anonymous allegations are considered when the evidence is specific enough to check. Vague accusations are not circulated to authors.
2. **Assess** whether the concern, if true, would be misconduct or an honest error. The AI preliminary report may be one input. A model's accusation is checked by a person before it is put to the authors.
3. **Ask the authors** for an explanation and for the raw data, original images, or approval documents, unless contacting them would create a serious risk (for example a risk of tampering that an institution should see first). The editor sets a deadline.
4. **Decide** whether the explanation answers the concern. The editor may ask a reviewer, a member of the board, or an independent expert who is told the files are confidential.
5. **Contact the institution** when the concern is serious (fabricated or falsified data, undisclosed AI use that undermines the results, plagiarism of substance, unethical research, or a non-response that leaves the concern standing). The editor does not run a disciplinary process in place of the employer or the university.
6. **Correct the record** as in section 11, and tell the person who raised the concern, in general terms, what was done. Confidential employment outcomes are not the journal's to publish.
7. **Keep the file.** Records of misconduct investigations are retained so that a later submission can be judged with the history in view.

The editor acts even if the authors withdraw the manuscript. Withdrawal during an integrity check does not close the check. The editor may still notify an institution and may refuse future submissions.

Malicious or knowingly false allegations are themselves misconduct. The journal does not publish the name of a complainant who acted in good faith.

## 11. Corrections, expressions of concern, and retractions

The version of record is corrected when it is wrong. The journal does not silently replace a PDF.

| Action | When it is used |
| --- | --- |
| **Correction** | A factual, numerical, authorship, funding, interest, or disclosure error that does not make the conclusions unreliable. This includes incomplete AI disclosure limited to language or formatting, when the data, citations, and interpretations remain reliable and were human-verified. The correction states what was wrong and what the AI statement should have said. |
| **Expression of concern** | The editor has a well-founded doubt and an investigation is pending, or the institution is unable or unwilling to examine a serious concern. It is a notice to readers, not a final scientific judgement. |
| **Retraction** | The findings are unreliable because of error or misconduct; the paper includes fabricated or falsified data or images; primary data or material interpretations were generated by a model and not human-verified; plagiarism or unethical research makes publication untenable; or duplicate publication requires it. |
| **Removal** | Only in the rare case where the law requires the text to be taken down (for example a court order, or a privacy breach that cannot be repaired by redaction). A removal notice stays on the article page. Bibliographic metadata remain where the law allows. |

Retraction notices:

- are linked to the article and are freely available;
- state who is retracting (the authors, the editor, or both);
- state the reason in enough detail for a reader to understand what failed;
- avoid defamatory detail that is not needed to correct the record;
- are themselves a citable item with their own DOI once the journal issues DOIs;
- do not disappear if the authors object. An author's disagreement is noted if they state one, and the editor retracts anyway when the record requires it.

A retracted article remains online, marked as retracted, so that citations can be followed. The HTML and PDF carry the notice.

Undisclosed AI use discovered after publication always produces a public notice: a correction or a retraction, chosen as above. There is no off-the-record fix.

Honest error is not misconduct. It is still corrected. The editor distinguishes the two in the notice when the facts allow it.

## 12. Complaints and appeals

**Appeals of a rejection** follow the [Peer review policy](peer-review-policy.md). One appeal, a different editor where possible, a final outcome.

**Complaints about process** — delay, a reviewer comment that is abusive, a suspected undisclosed conflict, a breach of confidentiality, or an AI check that sent the manuscript, the data, or the code to an external AI service — go to [EMAIL]. The editor-in-chief answers in writing. If the complaint is about the editor-in-chief, a designated board member who is not involved, [DEPUTY EDITOR NAME], answers and may ask the publisher, [PUBLISHER LEGAL NAME], to support an external review.

**Complaints about published content** are treated as allegations under section 10.

The journal does not respond to mass, templated demands that lack a specific scholarly concern.

## 13. Commercial conduct

The journal is gold open access. It does not charge a submission fee, or a fee for colour or speed. A fixed article processing charge (APC) of 1000 PLN is charged only after the article is accepted. The charge never influences the editorial decision, and editors and reviewers deciding on a paper do not see payment status. There are no fee waivers. There is no paid fast track and no paid guarantee of acceptance.

Reviewers who complete a review receive a discount voucher toward the APC of their own future article. The discount is set by the editorial office and published on the site.

Sponsorship of the journal's operations, if any, is disclosed on the website. Sponsors do not choose articles, reviewers, or decisions. The journal does not publish paid research articles. Notices that are advertisements, if they are ever accepted, are labelled and are not listed as research.

Special issues and guest editors use the same policies. A guest editor does not replace the human editor's final decision. The editor-in-chief may refuse a theme that is a vehicle for a product, a laboratory's unpublished backlog, or a paper mill.

Board members are listed with their affiliations and ORCIDs. A person who has not agreed to serve is not listed. The journal does not buy or borrow names for a masthead.
